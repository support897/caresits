How the menu works

Your sidebar shape, as the build carries it.

  • The menu hides when the app opens. The screen stays calm and full.
  • Tap Menu and the menu takes most of the screen, about three quarters of it. The app shrinks to a thin sliver at the edge.
  • Tap the sliver, or tap Close, and the app is full again.
  • The four groups open one at a time. Open one and the others close.
  • Nothing about the menu is remembered between visits. Every visit starts with the full screen app.

Finding care

Group one of the menu. Answer, look, match.

Questionnaire

The questions that build your profile and drive the matching.

Inside it

  • Step one: your country. One pick from 21 country packs, from Australia to Switzerland.
  • Then your side and your role. Families say the carer they are looking for. Carers pick from seven paths: Babysitter · Nanny · Au Pair, with a Demi Pair switch · Family Assistant · Newborn and Overnight · Governess and Tutor Nanny · Childminder and Family Day Care, which asks for registration proof.
  • Five pages of base questions a side: 23 for a family, 21 for a carer. Then the deeper levels open.
  • Every answer is a pick. Chips you tap: age as a pick list of years, pay as amount picks, distance as kilometre picks, experience as years per age band. Almost nothing is typed.
  • The only typed answers: your name, your postcode, and two write in answers a side at the Full level.
  • Family write ins: Tell us about each child in a few words · What would make a carer feel at home in your house.
  • Carer write ins: Why do you love working with children · What are you best at, and what do you find hard.
  • Most family questions carry a strength pick: Must have · I prefer · Either way. A must is a hard line. The app never loosens a must for you; it can only suggest, and only you can act.
  • The food safety list is picks only: Peanuts · Tree nuts · Dairy · Eggs · Gluten · Soy · Sesame · Fish · Shellfish · Lupin · Other foods · None.
  • Pay words follow your country pack: a wage for a nanny, pocket money for an au pair, with your country scheme note beside it.
  • Your draft saves itself as you go. Stop any time and come back.
  • The time promise at the welcome: about 10 minutes for everything, and about half that to reach Quick Connect and finish later.

The four levels

  • Explore: look at everything, free, no questions needed, no connecting.
  • Quick Connect: the base plus the safety and core set. Messaging, job posts and interest notes open here. Matches are labelled rough, never Perfect Pair.
  • Better Match: scores with reasons. Still never Perfect Pair.
  • Full Match: every match question answered. The only level that can earn Perfect Pair.
  • A pair is capped by the side that answered less. 100% can only ever mean all of the match questions answered, never a promise.
  • The cap you set stands: at most 50 questions a side. Family 50 · Carer 50. The rest are parked, by name, in the research file.

You ordered: question based matching, your musts as hard lines, picks not typing, four levels, a cap of 50 questions a side, postcode in place of suburb.

It is built: yes, all of it, counted in the build: Quick 25 · Better 47 · Full 20 · Optional 20 of 112, placed under the cap.

Gate: Quality PASS · Efficiency PASS · QA PASS on this build. The depth, the shapes and the cap were approved by you on 15 October.

  • A fit chip on every card: Perfect Pair · Strong Fit · Good Fit, with the pair score as a percent. The score is the average of both directions, yours for them and theirs for you.
  • Up to three reasons under each card, in plain words, from both sides of the match.
  • A quiet line on a card when a profile is quick: Quick profile, rough match.
  • A Message button on every card, and a quiet row under it: Report · Block.
  • The map: pins at postcode area level only, with a standing note on the panel that says so. Tap a pin and its card opens with distance and the same percent the list shows.
  • Exploring with no profile: you see the pins and an invite line, and no personal percent anywhere. A distance appears only once your own postcode is on file. The app never guesses where you are.
  • The nearly list, for families only: people who missed one must, with the missed must named in plain words. The carer is never told they missed.
  • No profile yet: a resting Bunny card walks you to the questionnaire, and the map still stands. Nobody matches: a calm empty card, never a dead end.

You ordered: browse everything free, the first five first, a map by area, match reasons you can read, family photos only for people you are matched with or talking to.
